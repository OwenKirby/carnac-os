# Stone Circle: the wavetable bank

15 banks × 8 morph frames, generated by `src/dsp/gen_wavetables.py`. The architecture is unchanged: 512-sample
Q15 cycles, 7 band-limited mip levels per frame, one gain per bank (morphing never changes level), all
stored as one image in Felucca's store flash. Only the waveforms are new.

**Theme.** Druidic and neolithic: standing stones, rituals, weathering. Each bank's morph is one process. Frame 1
is the stone before that process begins and frame 8 is the stone after it ends, so sweeping the scan knob plays the
process.

**Changing the set.** Rerun `python src/dsp/gen_wavetables.py`, upload `wavetables_image.bin` with
`src/hardware/wt_store_upload.py`, and rebuild the web emulator (`web/emu/build.sh`). The names set the image's set
id, so the firmware refuses an image from another bank list and plays the sine until the matching image is
uploaded.

| # | Bank | Group | Morph in one line |
|---|------|-------|-------------------|
| 0 | Menhir | Lithic Foundations | sine → mossy saw |
| 1 | Barrow | Lithic Foundations | rounded mound → hollow resonant square |
| 2 | Trilithon | Lithic Foundations | solid block (square) → two uprights (double pulse) |
| 3 | Ley Line | Lithic Foundations | root + octave → full octave/fifth drone stack |
| 4 | Solstice | Celestial Alignments | CZ resonance sweeping from harmonic 1 to 16 |
| 5 | Moonstone | Celestial Alignments | FM bell, index 0 → 7 |
| 6 | Star Chart | Celestial Alignments | prime-number partials appearing one by one |
| 7 | Druid Chant | Arcane Rituals | male vowels m-u-o-a-e-i |
| 8 | Overtone Rite | Arcane Rituals | throat-singing whistle stepping up harmonics 6 → 16 |
| 9 | Votive Choir | Arcane Rituals | two women's voices, oo → aah, ever breathier |
| 10 | Bone Flute | Arcane Rituals | breath → pure tone → overblown shriek |
| 11 | Erosion | Time & Decay | clean saw → 2-bit, 32-sample-hold terraces |
| 12 | Frost Shatter | Time & Decay | sine → aliased wavefold with chipped-out shards |
| 13 | Dust | Time & Decay | warm tone → drifting noise |
| 14 | Hearth | Hearth | banked embers (12 soft harmonics) → the fire caught (52, brighter slope); the 32-bank set's Warm Drone |

New banks go at the end of `BANK_DEFS`, so saved patches keep their bank numbers. Banks 0–13 share the original
dither stream and later banks draw their own (`DITHER_BANKS`), so adding one leaves the existing tables bit-identical.

Two notes for the whole set:

- **Noise is fixed per bank.** Ley Line, Bone Flute and Dust draw their noise once, so a sweep only changes it
  smoothly. A noise cycle repeats at the played pitch, so it sounds pitched and buzzy. These banks work best low.
- **The SINE is unchanged.** The sub-oscillator's SINE and the no-image fallback (`wavetable_sine`) are now built on
  their own at the old level (peak ~4926, about −16 dB) instead of being copied from bank 0, so the new bank 0
  cannot change the sub's level.

## Group 1: Lithic Foundations

Deep, heavy and dark, for drones and bass. All four are band-limited, and their energy sits in the low harmonics:
spectral centroid stays at or below harmonic 17.

### 0 Menhir
**Concept.** A standing stone being raised out of the earth.
**Design.** Additive saw in sine phase: harmonic *k* at `e^-(k-1)/c / k`. Raising *c* uncovers the series, and the
sine phases make the cycle lean into a saw's ramp. The fundamental grows from 1.0 to 1.5 so the stone keeps its
weight as it brightens.

| Frame | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Harmonics cleared (*c*) | 0, pure sine | 1.3 | 5.2 | 12 | 21 | 33 | 47 | 64 |
| Sounds like | buried stone | soft octave | warm organ | dark saw | | | | full mossy saw |

Frame 1 is the exact pure sine the pitch test reads.

### 1 Barrow
**Concept.** The long barrow: an earth mound over a stone chamber, dug open frame by frame.
**Design.** Odd harmonics only. At 1/k² in sine phase they draw a row of rounded mounds; at 1/k they draw a square.
A resonance between harmonics 3 and 7 (the chamber's boom) swells as the chamber opens. A moss low-pass rises from
harmonic 10 to 40.
**Frames.** Frame 1: rounded, nearly sine (1/k², low-passed at 10). Frames 2–4: slopes sharpen and the chamber
resonance appears at harmonics 3–4. Frames 5–7: square-like; the resonance climbs to harmonics 5–6 and the breach lets
even harmonics seep in. Frame 8: hollow, booming square (1/k) with its resonance at harmonic 7 and the evens at full.

### 2 Trilithon
**Concept.** Two uprights and a lintel, the Stonehenge sarsen.
**Design.** The cycle is drawn as the stones' silhouette (8× oversampled, then band-limited). A fixed low-pass at
harmonic 48 keeps the edges weathered.
**Frames.** Frame 1: one solid block, a square. Frames 2–4: a gap opens in the block's middle (up to 15 % of the
cycle) and is cut toward the ground; the square takes a notch and gains even harmonics. Frames 5–7: the gap widens
and drops below ground level. Frame 8: two narrow uprights over a trench, a double pulse that pulls the tone toward
its octave.

### 3 Ley Line
**Concept.** The earth current running between the stones.
**Design.** A drone stack of octaves and fifths only (harmonics 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32) at 1/k^0.7, so it
never clashes, with a low mossy hiss below harmonic 40. Phases are random but fixed per partial, so frames only add.
**Frames.** Frame 1: root and octave. Frame 2: + fifth. Frame 3: + double octave. Frame 4: + 6th harmonic (the fifth
above). Frames 5–7: 8th, 12th, 16th harmonics wake. Frame 8: the full stack to the 32nd, with hiss at its strongest.

## Group 2: Celestial Alignments

Bright, metallic, resonant. They start as near-sines and end with a centroid between harmonics 20 and 30.

### 4 Solstice
**Concept.** Midsummer sunrise through the heel stone: a narrow beam of light.
**Design.** Casio CZ-style resonance: a sine at *r* × pitch, restarted every cycle under a falling (saw) window. The
window is zero at the restart, so there is no click. That makes a resonant peak at harmonic *r* without a filter.
*r* rises exponentially, so each frame is an equal pitch step of the resonance.

| Frame | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Resonance (harmonic) | 1 | 1.5 | 2.2 | 3.3 | 4.9 | 7.3 | 10.8 | 16 |

Frame 1 is the sun below the horizon (almost a sine); frame 8 is the beam, a thin, ringing peak.

### 5 Moonstone
**Concept.** A ringing stone struck under the moon.
**Design.** Two-operator FM in the DX bell ratio 1:3.5: carrier 2 modulated at 7, so sidebands fall at 2, 5, 9, 12,
16, 19…. A hum tone at the fundamental keeps the bell on the played pitch. Drawn 8× oversampled, so it does not
alias. Level stays flat across the morph (FM keeps its power).

| Frame | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mod index | 0 | 0.6 | 1.4 | 2.3 | 3.4 | 4.5 | 5.7 | 7 |

Frame 1: hum plus a clean octave strike. Middle frames: a glassy bell. Frame 8: clangorous moonlight-on-wet-stone
sidebands.

### 6 Star Chart
**Concept.** Dusk to full night over the circle, the stars coming out one by one.
**Design.** The fundamental plus prime-numbered harmonics only (2, 3, 5, 7, 11 … 89) at 0.8/p^0.35, with fixed random
phases. Primes share no pattern, so together they ring metallic, almost inharmonic.
**Frames.** Frame 1: the octave alone (first star). Frame 2: 3rd and 5th. Frames 3–4: 7 to 17. Frames 5–7: the 20s
to 60s. Frame 8: all 24 primes to 89, glittering.

## Group 3: Arcane Rituals

Vocal and breathy. These banks move rather than simply brighten: formants are placed at a 110 Hz reference pitch, so
they sound most natural around A2.

### 7 Druid Chant
**Concept.** An invocation opening up, from a closed-mouth hum to a keening cry.
**Design.** Three Peterson & Barney style male formants on a Schroeder-phase harmonic source, with a breath of noise.
The formants glide along a vowel path, so every scan position is a vowel between two of these.
**Frames.** 1 *m* (hum) → 2 *m/u* → 3 *u/o* → 4 *o/a* → 5 *a* → 6 *a/e* → 7 *e/i* → 8 *i*.

### 8 Overtone Rite
**Concept.** Throat singing (khoomei): a melody drawn from one held note.
**Design.** A low male 'o' drone at reduced level, plus one very narrow peak (the whistle) on a single harmonic.
Frames are discrete: a stepped scan or step sequencer plays the tune, and a smooth sweep crossfades between
overtones, as a real singer does.

| Frame | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whistle on harmonic | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 |

That is a rising scale: fifth, ♭seventh, octave, ninth, major third, fifth, ♭seventh, octave (two octaves up).

### 9 Votive Choir
**Concept.** A dawn procession of women's voices.
**Design.** Female formants, doubled with the copy's formants shifted (two singers). Their spread widens from 2 % to
5 % as more voices join. Breath rises with the square of the scan, so it stays clean early and opens up late.
**Frames.** 1 soft *oo* → 2–3 *oo/o* → 4 *o* → 5–7 *o/aah* → 8 an open, airy *aah*.

### 10 Bone Flute
**Concept.** A neolithic bone flute brought to life, then overblown in ecstasy.
**Design.** A near-pure flute spectrum (strong fundamental, a little 2nd and 3rd harmonic) plus fixed breath noise
(harmonics 2–80, 1/√k). Tone body, overblow and breath are separate curves.
**Frames.** Frame 1: mostly air across the hole, the tone just catching. Frames 2–4: the tone firms up and the
breath falls away. Frame 4–5: a clean, nearly pure flute. Frames 6–7: overblowing; the octave rises and an edgy
third appears. Frame 8: the octave dominates, with fresh breath: a shriek.

## Group 4: Time & Decay

Eroded, aliased, noisy, fractured. Erosion and Frost Shatter are drawn directly at 512 samples, so whatever folds
back becomes part of the cycle (still periodic, still click-free). The mip levels still band-limit them as you play
higher.

### 11 Erosion
**Concept.** Centuries of weather on a carved face: the grain of the stone shows through.
**Design.** A sharp band-limited saw, then bit reduction and sample-and-hold rate reduction, with the aliasing kept:
the sound of an early sampler.

| Frame | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bits | clean | 6 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 2 |
| Hold (samples) | 1 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 8 | 8 | 16 | 32 |

Frame 8 is a few blocky terraces: 4 levels × 16 steps per cycle.

### 12 Frost Shatter
**Concept.** Freeze-thaw: water in the cracks expands until the stone splits.
**Design.** A sine into a triangle wavefolder. The drive rises from 1 to 8, and a growing offset (up to 0.4) makes
the folds lopsided (even harmonics: the crack). Drawn at 512 samples, so the fold corners alias.
**Frames.** Frame 1: a smooth sine (unbroken ice). Frames 2–5: the folds multiply, glassy and then crystalline.
Frame 6: the first shards chip out (short runs of 2–6 samples set to silence). Frames 7–8: up to 12 shards; the cycle
is fractured, harsh and glitchy. Shard positions are fixed per bank, so frames only add cracks.

### 13 Dust
**Concept.** The monolith returning to the earth.
**Design.** Equal-power crossfade from a warm tone (1/k^1.5, sine phase) to shaped noise, so the level holds through
the morph. Along the way the tone's harmonics are jittered and their phases scattered. The noise band rises from
harmonic 6 to 36, swells once per cycle (a gust), and a soft saturator wears down its tall peaks.
**Frames.** Frame 1: a solid, warm tone. Frames 2–4: the tone crumbles; harmonics wobble in level and lose their
shape, and dust begins to sift through. Frames 5–7: mostly grit, with the tone a ghost. Frame 8: only drifting dust
on a slow gust. Play it low.
